══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
                ZM ANOMALY — ALL MODELS MISCLASSIFIED                 
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

  Ticker: ZM (Zoom Video Communications)
  Ground truth direction : UP  (+113.2% Jan–Apr 2020)
  FinBERT sentiment score: -0.7473  (NEGATIVE)

  Model predictions:
    Graph Diffusion       : predicted DOWN  ✗ WRONG
    Random Forest         : predicted DOWN  (confidence: 51%)  ✗ WRONG
    GNN                   : predicted DOWN  (confidence: 100%)  ✗ WRONG

  Root cause analysis:
  ZM's FinBERT sentiment scored -0.75 (negative) due to concurrent
  security and privacy scandal headlines (Zoom-bombing, encryption
  disputes, banned by schools/governments). However, ZM's stock
  surged +113% over the same period driven by an unprecedented
  demand surge as remote work became mandatory globally.
  This is a textbook case of headline sentiment diverging from
  underlying economic fundamentals — the graph-level demand signal
  (WFH adoption, enterprise subscriptions) completely overwhelmed
  the negative tone of individual news articles.
  No model trained on news features alone can resolve this.

  Research implication:
  This anomaly is the central motivation for the graph-based
  propagation approach. A model that incorporates network-level
  signals (which sectors benefit structurally from remote work)
  would correctly classify ZM as a primary beneficiary regardless
  of its negative press coverage. Sentiment alone is insufficient.
